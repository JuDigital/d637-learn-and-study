Tap a lesson to open it. Each one explains a topic in plain words, with a picture or chart and the test traps to watch for.

★Start here: how it all connectsThe whole course on one page
⚡ 30 second versionStandard → Objective (condition, behavior, criterion) → Pre-assess → Teach with a strategy → Formative checks → Differentiate → Summative test → Use the data → Repeat.

This whole course is one loop. Almost every test question fits somewhere on it. When you read a scenario, ask yourself: what step is this teacher on?

  1. Pick the standardThe state or national standard: what students must learn this grade.
  2. Write a measurable objectiveCondition + behavior + criterion. It must match the standard.
  3. Plan the summative assessment firstBackward design: decide how you'll know they learned it before planning the lesson.
  4. Pre-assessFind out what students already know (readiness).
  5. Choose a strategyDirect, indirect, cooperative, discovery, or independent study.
  6. Teach and scaffoldI do, we do, you do. Support students in their ZPD, then fade the help.
  7. Check with formative assessmentExit tickets, thumbs up, partner talk. Adjust as you go.
  8. DifferentiateChange content, process, product, or environment based on readiness, interest, and profile. Be culturally responsive.
  9. Summative assessmentMeasure mastery at the end with a test or a rubric.
  10. Use the dataReteach, regroup, enrich, give feedback. Then the loop starts again.

1. Match the standard's verb (missed Q1)

Standard: “Classify real values as whole numbers, integers, rational, or irrational.” The right objective was “Classify a list of numbers as rational or irrational with at least 80% accuracy.” You picked “find their sum,” which is adding, a different skill.

Rule: Find the verb in the standard. Pick the objective with the SAME skill and a measurable criterion. Ignore choices that add a new skill, even if they sound harder or smarter.

4. Formative vs. summative (missed Q24, Q25)
Formative: “how well are they learning” during a unitQuick and low stakes.✓ Thumbs up, thumbs down survey. ✗ Not a project: projects are usually summative.
Summative: “assess mastery” after a unitAn individual exam or final product.✓ Short answer exam to interpret the book. ✗ Not a group discussion: that's formative.
Rule: Look for the clue words. “During,” “halfway,” “how well they're learning” = formative, so pick the quickest check. “Finished the unit,” “mastery” = summative, so pick the individual exam.
5. Differentiation details (missed Q33, Q34, Q35, Q36)
  • Benefit of differentiation: promoting a respectful environment, where every learner's needs are honored. NOT freeing students from performance pressure.
  • Environment differentiation: creating a welcoming, inclusive, supportive space. (Various ways to access material = process. Choice in showcasing learning = product.)
  • The 5 elements of a differentiated classroom: content, process, product, affect, and learning environment. Not technology, workstations, or class size.
  • Content accommodation: giving one student broken down instructions for the class worksheet. Same work, easier access.
Rule: Simplified objectives, fewer assignments, or simplified text with fewer words all change WHAT is learned. Those are modifications, not accommodations. You picked “simplified set of learning objectives,” which is a modification.

1Objectives aligned to standardsStandards, objectives, and the C B C formula
⚡ 30 second versionStandards = what students learn this year (state or national). Objectives = specific, measurable daily goals that build toward the standard. A good objective has a Condition, a Behavior, and a Criterion.
StandardBig, yearly, written by the state or nation.TEKS 1.8.C: describe the plot elements of a story.
ObjectiveSmall, daily, written by the teacher. Measurable.After a read aloud, students will describe the problem, 3 events, and resolution with 4 of 5 correct.
Build an objective: C + B + C
PartAsksExample
ConditionUnder what circumstances? With what tools?Without a calculator...
BehaviorWhat will students DO? (observable verb)...students will add two digit numbers...
CriterionHow well? What counts as mastery?...with 90% accuracy.
✓ Measurable verbsdescribe, list, identify, compare, solve, write, label, explain
✗ Not measurableunderstand, know, learn, appreciate, be aware of, enjoy
Planning the year
  • Scope and sequence: what gets taught (scope) and in what order and when (sequence).
  • Spiraling curriculum: topics come back each year, a little harder each time.
Test trap: If an answer choice says “students will understand...” it's not a measurable objective. Look for an action you can see and a number for mastery.
Test trap: The objective's verb should match the standard's verb. If the standard says “describe,” the objective should say “describe,” not “identify.”

Scaffolding is the temporary help you give inside the ZPD, like sentence stems, picture cards, and modeling. Just like scaffolding on a building, it comes down when the student can stand on their own.

Test trap: ZPD is Vygotsky, not Piaget. Vygotsky = learning is social, with a more knowledgeable other. Piaget = stages, child explores on their own.
Test trap: Behaviorism vs. cognitivism: behaviorism only looks at what you can SEE students do. Cognitivism looks INSIDE at how they think and remember.
4Assessment strategiesPre, formative, summative, rubrics
⚡ 30 second versionPre-assessment = before teaching (baseline). Formative = during (adjust teaching). Summative = end (measure mastery). Rubrics grade performance tasks with clear criteria and descriptors.
TypeWhenWhyExamples
Pre-assessmentBeforeFind the baselinePretest, screener, diagnostic, picture sort
FormativeDuringAdjust teachingExit ticket, thumbs up, whiteboards, partner talk
SummativeEndMeasure masteryUnit test, final exam, final project
Soup trick: When the cook tastes the soup, that's formative. When the guest tastes the soup, that's summative.
Performance-based
Performance-based learningTeaching so students can DO the skill.
Performance-based assessmentA task graded with a rubric that shows the skill.Your WGU Tasks 1, 2, and 3.
Scales vs. rubrics
ScaleBroad overall level.1 beginning, 2 developing, 3 proficient, 4 advanced.
RubricDetailed criteria with a descriptor for each level.Not Evident, Approaching Competence, Competent for each rubric row.
  • Descriptors explain what each level looks like, clearly enough for students to know how to improve.
  • Transparency and clarity: students see the criteria BEFORE they start, so grading is fair.
Test trap: It's not the tool, it's how you USE it. A quiz used to adjust tomorrow's lesson is formative. The same quiz used for a final grade is summative.
5Using assessment data to planData, feedback, and the teaching cycle
⚡ 30 second versionData is any information that guides teaching. Collect it, analyze it, then reteach, regroup, or enrich. Good feedback is timely, specific, and actionable.
The data cycle
  1. AssessPre-assessment, exit tickets, observations, work samples, tests.
  2. AnalyzeWho got it? Who didn't? What mistake are they making?
  3. AdjustReteach in a small group, regroup students, enrich students who are ready, change the strategy.
  4. ReassessCheck again to see if the change worked.
What the data says, what you do
The data shows...Best next step
Most of the class missed itReteach the whole class with a different strategy
A small group missed itSmall group reteach (flexible grouping)
Some already mastered itEnrichment or extension
Everyone mastered itMove on
Feedback that works
✓ Effective“Your first two events are in order. Check event 3. What happened after Tad helped?”
✗ Not effective“Good job!” or “Wrong.” Praise isn't feedback. It doesn't tell the student what to do next.
Test trap: When a question gives you data, the right answer almost always USES it to change instruction for the specific students who need it. Moving on or retesting everyone is rarely right.
6Principles of differentiationReadiness, interest, profile, and content, process, product, environment
⚡ 30 second versionDifferentiate based on readiness, interest, and learning profile. Differentiate what you change: content, process, product, or environment. Same goal, different paths.
Based on WHO the student is (R I P)
ReadinessCurrent skill level for this goal.Found with pre-assessment.
InterestWhat the student cares about.Found with interest surveys.
Learning profileHow the student learns best.Visual, movement, alone, in a group.
WHAT the teacher can change
ElementAsksExample
ContentWHAT are they learning? (KUD: know, understand, do)Texts at different reading levels on the same topic
ProcessHOW do they make sense of it?Manipulatives, graphic organizers, partner work
ProductHOW do they show it?Poster, video, essay, or oral retell

Cooking trick: Content = the ingredients. Process = the cooking. Product = the meal on the plate. Environment = the kitchen and the vibe.
Test trap: Differentiation does NOT mean a different standard for each student. Everyone works toward the same goal through different paths.
Test trap: Process vs. product: if students are still LEARNING it, that's process. If they're SHOWING what they learned, that's product.

8Culturally responsive teachingSeeing students' cultures as strengths
⚡ 30 second versionCulturally responsive teaching values students' customs, experiences, and perspectives and uses them to improve instruction. It's asset based: a student's culture is a strength, never a problem to fix.
Culturally responsive teachingThe approach: acknowledge and value students' cultures, experiences, and perspectives.Geneva Gay is the name linked to this term.
Culturally responsive pedagogy (CRP)The method: students learn differently, and that's connected to family, cultural identity, language, and background.Gloria Ladson-Billings wrote about culturally relevant pedagogy.
What it looks like
  • Books and examples that reflect students' lives and communities
  • Building relationships with students and families
  • Drawing on funds of knowledge, the skills students bring from home
  • Welcoming home languages
  • High expectations for every student
  • Learning about each student as an individual
✗ Deficit thinking“Her family doesn't speak English, so she's behind.”
✓ Asset-based thinking“She's bilingual. Let's use that strength.”
Test trap: Any answer that blames a student's culture, family, or language is wrong. Any answer built on stereotypes is wrong. Pick the answer that builds relationships and uses students' backgrounds as strengths.
